Countersign: Approvals & Sign-off for Jira

An approval that actually blocks the transition, in any Jira project, on any plan. Multi-stage and parallel approvals, quorum rules, delegation, escalation, and a tamper-evident audit trail you can export and check for yourself. One edition, everything included, US$0.65 per user per month, free for teams of ten or fewer.

Why this exists

Jira added a native approval step in October 2025. It is a genuine improvement and for some teams it is enough. Here is what it does not do, in Atlassian’s own words and their own users’:

  • It is Premium and Enterprise only. Atlassian’s documentation: “The approvals feature is only available on Premium editions in Jira.” On Free and Standard there is nothing.
  • It bypasses your workflow validators, by design. Atlassian’s knowledge base: “Approval steps bypass workflow transitions… it won’t be possible to enforce the validator on the same transition as the approval step.” A commenter on Atlassian’s own launch post put it more plainly: “As long as approvals bypass the validators, this is just another 80% solution.”
  • Project roles cannot be approvers. The most-liked comment on that launch post is “Why can’t Roles be used to define approvers?”
  • An approver can add themselves. Atlassian’s troubleshooting page: “Anyone in the project with Edit any work item access can manage preset approvers from the work item.”
  • There is nowhere to say why you rejected something, and each stage needs its own custom field.

Countersign is the version that holds. It puts a real workflow validator on the transition, so the gate applies from the work item view, from a bulk change and from the REST API alike — and it works on Standard.

What it does

Approvals that are shaped like your organisation

  • Stages, sequential or parallel. Stages with the same round number run at the same time; rounds run in order. One mechanism, so the two cannot disagree at the edges.
  • Approvers by rule, not by list. A named person, everyone in a group, everyone in a project role, whoever is in a user-picker field on the work item, the reporter, the assignee, the project lead, the lead of each component, or the reporter’s or assignee’s manager.
  • Five quorum rules. Everyone must approve; any one is enough; any N of M; a percentage; or weighted, where the finance director’s approval carries five points and everybody else’s carries one.
  • Separation of duties. Nobody approves a request they raised, unless you say otherwise, and you can require a different person at every stage.
  • You choose what a rejection means. It can end the approval, send it back one stage, or send it back to the beginning for rework — and a stage can either stop at the first rejection or keep collecting until approval has become arithmetically impossible.

Enforcement, not decoration

Four workflow validators, three of which need no configuration at all:

  • Approval has been granted — blocks the transition until every approval on the work item is in.
  • No approval is still waiting.
  • An approval has been started.
  • Only a current approver may make this transition.

They sit on the transition, so they apply to the issue view, a bulk transition and a REST call equally. And they fail closed: if the approval cannot be read, the transition is refused with an explanation rather than waved through. A gate that opens when it is confused is worse than no gate, because you believe it is there.

A workflow post function starts, restarts, sends back or cancels an approval on a transition, and the same operations are a first-class action in the Jira Automation rule builder.

Delegation, cover and escalation

  • Hand a pending approval to a colleague. They take your seat rather than being added beside it, so the number of approvals still needed does not move.
  • Set out-of-office cover; it applies when a stage opens, and the trail records who covered for whom. If your cover is already an approver on that stage, the seat stays with you — cover must never quietly halve a vote.
  • Remind after N hours, then every N after that. A reminder sweep that missed a week sends one reminder, not seven.
  • Escalate after N hours by notifying, adding approvers, auto-approving or auto-rejecting — measured in working hours if you want, so a Friday-afternoon deadline does not expire on Saturday with nobody at work. A stage closed by an escalation says so, and is never presented as somebody’s signature.

A trail that is worth having

Every approval is an append-only log. Nothing is ever edited: changing your mind appends, sending an approval back appends, and the decisions from round one sit beside round two in the export.

Each entry commits to the digest of the entry before it, and the first entry commits to the work item and the policy it is about. Remove an entry, reorder two, edit a comment, or lift a whole valid trail onto a different work item, and the chain stops verifying. The panel re-checks it every time you open it, and the CSV export includes the digests so your auditor can check it outside Jira — the digest is an ordinary SHA-256 over a canonical JSON form, described in our documentation, not a private format.

Each decision records who, when, from which stage, why, what they were asked to agree to, and a snapshot of the work item as it stood at that moment. If the work item changes afterwards, the app says so on the work item.

Sign-off

A stage can require the approver to type their own name — checked against the name on their Atlassian account — and to give a reason. The statement they agreed to is stored verbatim with the decision, inside the digest.

What we do not claim. We do not describe this as a legally binding electronic signature, and we make no claim of eIDAS, ESIGN Act or 21 CFR Part 11 compliance. A Forge app cannot re-authenticate you at the moment of signing — no app on Jira Cloud can — so we tell you exactly what is recorded and let your compliance team decide whether it is enough: the Atlassian-authenticated account behind the request, the account id Atlassian attests to, the name typed, the statement agreed to, the time, the state of the work item, and a hash chain over all of it.

Finding and reporting

  • Ordinary JQL. approvalStatus = APPROVED, approvalPendingWith ~ currentUser(), approvalWaiting = 1, approvalApprovedBy, approvalStage, approvalRounds, approvalStarted, approvalDecided, approvalOutstanding, approvalName. Build your own filters, boards and gadgets without us.
  • A “My approvals” page, two dashboard gadgets, and a reporting view with cycle time, which stage holds things up, who was rostered and never answered, and the first-pass rate — how often an approval got through without being sent back.
  • CSV export of the report and of the full audit trail.

Notifications, honestly

A Forge app cannot send email. So Countersign does not pretend to: it asks your Jira to send its own notification, from your address, obeying your notification scheme; it posts a Jira comment naming the approver, the stage and the decision, so it lands in the Activity stream people already read; and it keeps an in-app queue on the “My approvals” page. Nothing leaves Atlassian, and no message ever arrives from a domain your staff do not recognise.

Runs on Atlassian

Countersign declares no external network access of any kind — no fetch, no egress, no remote services, no servers of ours. Your approval records, audit trail, policies and out-of-office windows live in Forge storage inside your own site, in your own data residency region. There is nothing to breach at our end because there is no our end.

None of the established apps in this category holds the Runs on Atlassian badge. This one is built to.

What it does not do

We would rather you heard this from us.

  • You cannot approve from email or from Slack. Both need the app to reach outside Atlassian, which would forfeit Runs on Atlassian, and inbound email is not something Forge offers at all. Approvers need a Jira licence and approve in Jira.
  • There is no password or biometric re-authentication at signing. No Jira Cloud app has it; the platform does not expose it. See the sign-off section above for exactly what is recorded instead.
  • The trail is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. A chain proves an entry has not been altered since it was written. It is not a notary and it is not a digital signature, and we will not tell you it is.
  • Approval events cannot be written into Jira’s History tab, which is Jira’s own field-change log and closed to apps. The comment stream is the nearest thing and we use it.

Getting started

  1. Install it. Nothing appears anywhere until a project turns it on.
  2. Project settings › Approvals → turn it on, then pick one of three ready-made policies: one approver, two stages, or any two of three. Change whatever you like.
  3. Type a work item key into the preview to see exactly who each stage resolves to, and why, before it ever runs.
  4. Add Approval has been granted to the transition you want to gate.
