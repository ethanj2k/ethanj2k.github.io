Pivot Reports for Jira

Tagline: Pivot tables, charts and time-in-status over any JQL — live, timezone-correct, and a third of the price.

Summary

Pivot Reports turns a JQL query, a board, a sprint or a saved filter into a pivot table and a chart in three clicks: pick a row field, an optional column field, and one or more measures. Count issues, sum or average story points, time spent, estimates or any number field, take a median or an 85th percentile, or write a small formula like sum(points) / count — broken down by status, assignee, priority, type, epic, sprint, version, component, label, any select/user/date custom field, or by created/resolved date per day, week, month, quarter or year. Every cell drills down to the issues behind it.

Ask how long work actually sits in each status, or what the board looked like on a date three months ago, and get an answer read live from Jira's own change history — no import, no cube, no query language to learn. The app reads Jira as you, so what you see is exactly what your permissions allow, and nothing leaves Atlassian's infrastructure (Runs on Atlassian).

Highlights

  • Any two fields, any measures. Rows × columns pivot with row subtotals, column totals and a grand total that always reconcile. Up to six measures side by side: count, sum, average, min, max, median, 75th/85th/90th/95th percentile.
  • Calculated measures. A small, safe formula language over those aggregations — sum(customfield_10016) / count, count - sum(x). Evaluated from each cell's own totals, so a subtotal is the true weighted figure, never an average of averages. No MDX, nothing to execute, nothing to hire a consultant for.
  • Time in status. How long each issue spent in each status, from the changelog, with percentiles and an optional window. Durations always reconcile with the issue's own age, at every level of the table.
  • As of a date. Rebuild the report as it stood at the end of any past day — status, assignee, priority, labels, components, versions, story points — from each issue's history, in your timezone. Issues created later are left out.
  • Percent views. Any report as % of total, % of row or % of column.
  • Time buckets that are actually right. Dates are bucketed in your Jira timezone, not the server's, by day, ISO week, month, quarter or year — including across daylight-saving changes.
  • Fifteen chart types. Bar, grouped bar, stacked bar, 100% stacked, horizontal bar, horizontal stacked, line, area, pie, donut, funnel, tile, gauge, table, and a table heat map — drawn with Atlassian's own components, so they match your Jira theme.
  • Conditional formatting. Colour cells by thresholds you set, or a heat map fitted to the numbers on screen. Follows light and dark themes.
  • Cross-project. Report on one project from its page, or across every project you can see, with one checkbox.
  • Sources beyond JQL. A board, a sprint, a saved filter or a JQL query — and any of them narrowed further by your own JQL.
  • Quick filters. Define named filters on a report; readers pick one on the page or on a dashboard to narrow it. A quick filter can only ever narrow, never widen.
  • Curate the values. Rename values (two renamed alike read as one row), hide the ones you do not want, and pin the order that matters.
  • Drill-down. Click any cell, subtotal or total to list the issues in it.
  • Saved reports, in folders. Keep reports private, or share them with everyone who can browse the project — or only with chosen project roles. Every check runs on the server, on every open.
  • Dashboard gadget. Put any saved or ad-hoc report on a Jira dashboard, quick filters and all.
  • Export. CSV with RFC 4180 quoting and formula-injection protection, and PNG or PDF of the table and chart together.
  • Scheduled snapshots. A project admin can have a shared report refreshed daily and, if they choose, have Jira notify the report's audience.
  • Complete data. The JQL is paged to exhaustion (up to 5,000 issues per report, 1,500 for reports that read history) with a clear warning when a query exceeds that — never just the first page.
  • Almost entirely read-only. Scopes: read:jira-work, read:jira-user, storage:app, and write:jira-work used for exactly one thing — asking Jira to send the optional scheduled notification. The app never creates, edits, transitions, comments on or deletes an issue.

What it is not

Pivot Reports is the deterministic core of a BI tool, not a data warehouse. It does not import your data into a cube, has no MDX, does not join other data sources (SQL, Google Sheets, Tempo), and cannot publish a report to the public web. If you need those, eazyBI is the right tool — at its price. See COMPETITORS.md for the full comparison, including what is impossible for an app that never sends your data anywhere.
