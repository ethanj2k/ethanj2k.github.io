A cheaper alternative to Timepiece (Time in Status) for Jira Cloud

Status Clock: Time in Status & SLA for Jira measures how long work items spend in each status on a working calendar, and runs SLAs with start, pause and stop conditions, breach detection and JQL search. It costs US$0.62 per user per month for 11–100 users, against US$1.20 for OBSS's Timepiece, and it is free for up to 10 users.

Timepiece is a well-rated, feature-rich app with over 4,000 installs and a 4.8-star rating. This page sets out where the two differ, including where Status Clock is weaker.

Where Status Clock is weaker

  • No alerts outside Jira. Status Clock sends no email, Slack or Teams message and has no scheduled reports; that would mean sending data outside Atlassian. It can add a label and a comment on breach, and Jira Automation can take it from there.
  • No REST API, Power BI or eazyBI connector. The data leaves through JQL, the issue property and CSV export.
  • CSV only. No native Excel or PDF export.
  • No AI report builder, and English only.
  • Some settings are edited as text. Calendars, phases, SLAs and access rules have forms; teams and which calendar applies to which project are set in the configuration text on the admin page, which is checked before it is saved.
  • No duration-threshold highlighting yet. SLA standing is highlighted everywhere, but you can't yet set a rule such as “highlight anything over two days in Review”.
  • Sorting is one column at a time in the report.
  • Maturity and assurance. Timepiece has years of customers behind it, Cloud Fortified and ISO 27001. Status Clock is new.

Common complaints in Timepiece's reviews, and how Status Clock handles them

From Timepiece's Marketplace reviews and documentation, read in September 2026. Most criticism of Timepiece appears in otherwise positive 4-star reviews, and we have left out complaints its listing now answers.

What reviewers and documentation raisedWhat Status Clock does
“During Daylight Saving Time changes, the date calculations can become inaccurate, requiring manual adjustments” (2025).All day and hour arithmetic happens on the local wall clock, never by adding milliseconds to an instant, and it is tested across DST transitions against a minute-by-minute count.
Time in business hours shown in a format that is “impossible to total” without a spreadsheet formula (2021); a request for averages and totals along the bottom (2025).Every table has a summary row, and exports carry plain numbers in the unit you choose, to three decimals.
The screen rounds to two decimals while exports carry full precision, so the two can disagree (in their documentation).The panel, gadget, report and export are all calculated by one engine from the same measurement; only the display format differs.
Calendars, access settings and saved parameter sets don't carry over when you move sites (in their documentation).The whole configuration can be copied out as text and pasted back in on another site.
A 60-second limit on large reports, with exact limits not published.Reports are measured one page of work items at a time with progress shown, and every cap is a stated number shown when it applies.
Data processed outside your site's data residency region.Status Clock runs entirely on Atlassian's Forge platform and stores its settings in your site's own Forge storage.

Switching

Both apps read the history Jira already keeps, so Status Clock shows numbers for past work as soon as it is installed; there is no data to move. You will need to recreate calendars, status groups and SLAs. Run both side by side for a week and compare the numbers.
