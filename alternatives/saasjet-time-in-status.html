A cheaper alternative to SaaSJet's Time in Status for Jira Cloud

Status Clock: Time in Status & SLA for Jira measures how long work items spend in each status on a working calendar, and runs SLAs with start, pause and stop conditions, breach detection and JQL search, in one app. It costs US$0.62 per user per month for 11–100 users, against US$1.15 for SaaSJet's Time in Status, and it is free for up to 10 users.

SaaSJet's Time in Status is one of the most installed apps in the category, with over 5,000 installs and a 4.5-star rating, and it has features Status Clock lacks. This page sets out where the two differ, including where Status Clock is weaker.

Where Status Clock is weaker

  • Fewer kinds of report. No sprint velocity, burndown or carryover reports, no forecasting and no multi-site reports.
  • No REST API, Confluence export or Rovo agents. The data leaves through JQL, the issue property and CSV export.
  • CSV only. No native Excel, PDF or Google Sheets export.
  • No alerts outside Jira. On a breach it can add a label and a comment, which Jira Automation can act on; it sends no email of its own.
  • Some settings are edited as text. Teams and which calendar applies to which project are set in the configuration text on the admin page, which is checked before it is saved.
  • Sorting is one column at a time, and there is no duration-threshold highlighting yet.
  • English only.
  • Maturity and assurance. SaaSJet has years of customers behind it, Cloud Fortified and SOC 2. Status Clock is new.

Common complaints in Time in Status's reviews, and how Status Clock handles them

From Time in Status's Marketplace reviews, Atlassian Community threads and SaaSJet's documentation, read in September 2026. Much of the criticism is in otherwise positive 4-star reviews.

What reviewers and documentation raisedWhat Status Clock does
“It automatically updates when you change the config, and that can take ~30 seconds every time you change something” (2024); “Too slow and lagging” (2025).Work items are fetched once. Changing the grouping, metric, statistic, unit, sort or chart type redraws from what is already loaded; only a new query, date window or calendar fetches again.
Large reports hitting a computation time limit (25 seconds, in their documentation).Reports are measured one page of work items at a time, and the table fills in as each page arrives, so no single request runs near the platform's limit.
“It isn't clear on how values are getting calculated” (2025).The panel on each work item lists every status segment with its start, end, the calendar applied and its duration, and the segments add up to the total shown.
Time in status that “keeps increasing when we change an issue status after the business hours” (Atlassian Community, 2024).Time outside the working calendar counts as zero, by construction and by test.
A work item moved between projects that share status names is counted under both projects (in their documentation).Everything is keyed by status id, not status name.
Shared “public” presets can expose information from restricted projects (in their FAQ).A shared view stores a query and settings, never data. Every read runs as the person viewing it, so each reader sees only work items they can already see.
Needing a second app for SLAs.SLAs are part of Status Clock at no extra cost.

Switching

Both apps read the history Jira already keeps, so Status Clock shows numbers for past work as soon as it is installed; there is no data to move. You will need to recreate calendars, status groups and any SLAs. Run both side by side for a week and compare the numbers.
